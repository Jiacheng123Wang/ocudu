## 0. THE KEY QUESTION, ANSWERED

| Detector | Output (verified) | Calibrated LLRs? | Channel decoder in loop | Metric / numbers |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **DetNet** (SPAWC'17, TSP'19) | **soft symbol estimates**; soft mode = **approximate symbol posterior probabilities** `P(x=s\|y)` — *not* LLRs | no | **No** ("a full iterative decoding scheme is outside the scope of this paper") | uncoded BER; soft output scored by `δ(P,Q)=Σ_s\|P(s)−Q(s)\|`; runtime table |
| **OAMP-Net** (GlobalSIP'18) | soft symbol estimates | no | no | BER (uncoded) |
| **OAMP-Net2** (TSP'20) | **soft symbol estimate** `E{x\|r,τ}`; *plus* an LLR read-out Eq. (29) | LLR formula given, **calibration never discussed** | claimed in principle (soft-in/soft-out turbo) but **"experimental results are outside the scope of this paper and will be conducted in the future"** | BER vs OAMP/LMMSE/DetNet |
| **MMNet** (TWC'20 / ICASSP'20) | soft symbols internally → **hard symbol decisions**; **no LLR anywhere** | n/a | **No** — zero occurrences of LLR/LDPC/channel decoder/BER | **SER@10⁻³** only |
| **CMDNet** (TCOM'21) | soft outputs (LLRs) | **documents that DetNet's "soft" output is not soft**: "DetNet mostly provides hard decisions with ∼97% LLRs being −1 and 1" | **Yes** — 128×64 rate-1/2 LDPC, BP 10 iterations, Coded Frame Error Rate | coded FER |
| **Neural receivers / demappers** (LINE B) | **per-bit LLRs** | treated as a first-class problem (LLR correction, learned quantisation, mismatched-LLR warnings) | **Yes** — standard 5G-NR LDPC | BLER/TBLER, latency |

**Headline:** the model-driven MIMO detectors are **symbol-estimate detectors scored by uncoded BER/SER under perfect CSI**;
where they emit "soft" output it is a **symbol posterior**, not a per-bit LLR, and the one paper that measured it (CMDNet)
found DetNet's soft output collapses to near-hard decisions. Decoder-grade LLRs, their scaling/clipping, and the LDPC
interface are a **LINE-B** achievement.

## 1. LINE A

**Deep MIMO Detection** — IEEE SPAWC 2017, DOI 10.1109/SPAWC.2017.8227772 — [arXiv:1706.01151](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.01151) · [IEEE 8227772](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/8227772)
N. Samuel, T. Diskin, A. Wiesel. The DetNet origin paper ("DETNET was first proposed in 'Deep MIMO detection'"). Unfolds projected gradient descent into a network; output is a symbol estimate. Simulation-only. *(The task's "2017 IEEE SPL" attribution is wrong — the 2017 record is SPAWC.)*

**Learning to Detect** — IEEE Transactions on Signal Processing, vol. 67, no. 10, p. 2554, 2019, DOI 10.1109/TSP.2019.2899805 — [arXiv:1805.07631](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.07631) · [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1805.07631) · [ADS](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019ITSP...67.2554S/abstract)
The DetNet journal paper. **[full text]** Architecture: *"The structure of DetNet is obtained by unfolding the iterations of a projected gradient descent algorithm into a network"*; layers `q₁=y`, `q_{k+1}=ρ(W_k q_k + …)` on the **one-hot** representation `x_oh`; a ResNet-style residual skip is added. **Loss:** `l = Σ_{l=1}^{L} log(l)·‖x_oh − x̂_{oh,l}‖²` (the FullyCon baseline uses plain `l₂`). **OUTPUT TYPE:** hard-decision mode gives symbol/one-hot estimates; **§IV "Soft decision output"** states the detector must provide *"soft posterior distributions Prob(x_j = s_i | y)"*, and shows `argmin E[‖x_oh−x̂_oh‖²|y] = E[x_oh|y] = Prob(x = s|y)` so the one-hot output *"will provide the exact posterior probabilities"*. These are **symbol-level posteriors — not per-bit LLRs** — and the paper adds *"it also needs to allow additional soft inputs but we leave this for future work"*, i.e. **no decoder feedback / no iterative decoding**: *"Implementing a full iterative decoding scheme is outside the scope of this paper, and we only provide initial results on the accuracy of our posterior estimates."*
**Numbers [full text]:** soft output scored by `δ(P,Q)=Σ_s|P(s)−Q(s)|` vs exact posteriors, benchmarked against M-Best sphere decoding (M=5,7): BPSK over a 10×20 real channel (DetNet 30 and 50 layers, beats M-Best), 16-QAM over 4×8 complex (comparable to M-Best), 8-PSK over 4×8 (comparable only at high SNR). Hard decision: fixed-channel case over a 0.55-Toeplitz channel, varying-channel and soft-output cases over i.i.d. 𝒩(0,1) 30×60 channels; baselines ZF, AMP, SDR, SD; BPSK/QPSK/8-PSK and larger. Runtime (Table I, 30×60 FC): batch 1 → DetNet 0.0045 s vs SDR 0.009, AMP 0.005, SD 0.001, FullyCon 0.0004 s; batch 1000 → DetNet 1.1×10⁻⁴ s vs AMP 3×10⁻⁴, SDR 9×10⁻³. Training ≈3 days on an Intel i7-6700. **Zero occurrences of "one-bit", "1-bit", "quantiz", "ADC", "low-resolution" in the full text.**

Unfolded detection for low-resolution (incl. 1-bit) receivers; also at ISIT 2021 as "Model-Inspired Deep Detection with Low-Resolution Receivers".
**Binary MIMO Detection via Homotopy Optimization and Its Deep Adaptation** — IEEE TSP, DOI 10.1109/TSP.2020.3048232 — [IEEE 9311778](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/9311778). ML MIMO detection under one-bit quantised observations, binary symbols, deep-adapted.
**Detection and Channel Equalization with Deep Learning for Low Resolution MIMO Systems** — Asilomar (ACSSC) 2018, DOI 10.1109/ACSSC.2018.8645551.
**Accelerated and Deep Expectation Maximization for One-Bit MIMO-OFDM Detection** — IEEE TSP 2024, DOI 10.1109/TSP.2024.3359083 — [arXiv:2210.03888](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.03888).
**Deep Learning for Estimation and Pilot Signal Design in Few-Bit Massive MIMO Systems** — [IEEE 9847603](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/abstract/document/9847603) · [arXiv:2107.11958](http://export.arxiv.org/pdf/2107.11958) (explicit b-bit quantiser `Q_b(r)`).
**Regularized Neural Detection for One-Bit Massive MIMO** — [arXiv:2305.15543](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2305.15543); **Learning-Based One-Bit ML Detection: Dithering-Aided Adaptive** — [arXiv:2304.07696](http://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.07696v2); **Deep Signal Recovery with One-Bit Quantization** (ICASSP 2019) and **Model-Based Deep Learning for One-Bit Compressive Sensing** (IEEE TSP, [9187438](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/9187438)) — the last two are the most likely source of the mis-remembered "one-bit TSP 2019".

**A Model-Driven Deep Learning Network for MIMO Detection** — **IEEE GlobalSIP 2018** (not GLOBECOM) — [SIGPORT PDF](https://sigport.org/sites/default/files/docs/IEEE_Globalsip_2018.pdf) · [arXiv:1809.09336](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1809.09336) · [IEEE 8646357](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/8646357)
He, Wen, Jin, Li — OAMP-Net. **[full text]** Unfolded OAMP with learned per-layer step `r_t = x̂_t + γ_t W_t(y − H x̂_t)` and `x̂_{t+1} = η_t(r_t)`; **output = soft symbol estimates**; 2T learnable parameters. The same group tested trained parameters at 64-QAM/40 dB in **WINNER II** channels. No LLRs, no decoder.

**Model-Driven Deep Learning for MIMO Detection** — IEEE Transactions on Signal Processing, vol. 68, pp. 1702–1715, 2020, DOI 10.1109/TSP.2020.2976585 — [arXiv:1907.09439 v2](https://browse.arxiv.org/abs/1907.09439v2) · [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1907.09439) · [IEEE 9018199](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/9018199) · [ADS](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020ITSP...68.1702H/abstract)
This is **OAMP-Net2** — **IEEE TSP 2020, not JSTSP**. *(Traceable cause of the confusion: arXiv 1907.09439v1 was titled "Model-Driven Deep Learning for **Joint MIMO Channel Estimation and Signal Detection**"; v2 was retitled "Model-Driven Deep Learning for MIMO Detection".)* **[full text]** **OUTPUT TYPE:** layer output is the conditional mean `x̂_{d,t+1}^{(i)} = E{x_i | r_i, τ_t}` (soft symbol estimate) — and **§IV-C gives an explicit per-bit LLR read-out**: `L_A(b_{j,k}) = log( Σ_{S_j⁺} P(x_j|r_t,τ_t) / Σ_{S_j⁻} P(x_j|r_t,τ_t) )` (Eq. 29), with *"After interleaved and delivered to the channel encoder, extrinsic LLR can be computed and given to the OAMP-Net2 detector as updated prior information. The detector and decoder iteratively exchange information until convergence."* It contrasts itself with DetNet: *"Different from the DetNet in [19] that only can provide the soft-output, our proposed detector are the soft-input and soft-output receiver and therefore can achieve the turbo equalization."* **BUT:** *"We only provide the principle of the OAMP-Net2-based turbo receiver and the specific experimental results are outside the scope of this paper and will be conducted in the future."* → **no coded results in the paper, and no LLR calibration/scaling discussion.**
**Numbers [full text]:** 4 trainable parameters per layer `Ω_t=(γ_t,φ_t,ξ_t,θ_t)` → **4T** total (OAMP-Net: 2T). Table I: complexity OAMP-Net2 `O(TN_t³)`, DetNet `O(TN_t²)`, DNN-dBP/DNN-MPD/TPG/LcgNet `O(TN_rN_t)`; learnable variables — OAMP-Net2 4T, DetNet `(6N_rN_t+2N_r+N_t)T`, DNN-dBP `T(N_r+N_t)`, DNN-MPD 2T, LcgNet `4N_tT`. Channels: i.i.d. Rayleigh, **correlated Rayleigh via the Kronecker model** `H_c=R_R^{1/2} G R_T^{1/2}` (exponential correlation), and **3GPP 3D MIMO (TR 36.873) via QuaDRiGa** — where *"OAMP-Net2 indeed has performance loss… because it requires the channel matrix to be unitarily-invariant while the practical channel is very ill-conditioned"*, and *"only 4 trainable parameters in each layer may restrict the ability to adapt to realistic 3GPP MIMO channels"*, though it *"still outperforms the OAMP and LMMSE detectors"*. Sizes 4×4 and 8×8, QPSK/16-QAM; **perfect CSI** (*"all detectors are investigated with accurate CSI"*). Simulation-only.

**Adaptive Neural Signal Detection for Massive MIMO (MMNet)** — IEEE Transactions on Wireless Communications, vol. 19, no. 8, pp. 5635–5648, 2020, DOI 10.1109/TWC.2020.2996144 — [arXiv:1906.04610](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.04610) · [Nokia Bell Labs](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/publications-and-media/publications/adaptive-neural-signal-detection-for-massive-mimo/) · [MIT thesis](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/152866)
Khani, Alizadeh, Hoydis, Fleming. **[full text]** Per layer: learned complex linear operator `Θ_t^(1)` (N_t×N_r) + per-symbol noise-variance scaling `θ_t^(2)` (N_t×1), followed by the **model-based posterior-mean denoiser** `η_t(z_t)=E[x|z_t]` (not a learned black box); **10 layers**; total `2N_t(N_r+1)` trainable parameters (published text: 20K–41K). **OUTPUT TYPE: soft symbol estimates internally, hard symbol decisions externally — no LLRs, no bits.** Independent confirmations: the Nokia follow-up HyperMIMO writes *"MMNet consists of T layers… and a hard decision… to predict the final estimate x̂. One could also use r_x^(T) to predict bit-wise log likelihood ratios (LLRs)"* (i.e. LLR output is an unexplored option), and CMDNet states *"One major drawback of the latter approaches is that they focus on MIMO detection and do not provide soft outputs."* **Full-text keyword scan: 0 occurrences of LLR, log-likelihood, LDPC, "channel decoder"; 0 occurrences of the word BER — MMNet is evaluated by SER only** (`"the SNR required to achieve an SER of 10⁻³ as the primary performance metric"`). Loss: `L = (1/T)Σ_t ‖x̂_t − x‖₂²`.
**Numbers [full text]:** *"On i.i.d. Gaussian channels, MMNet requires two orders of magnitude fewer operations than existing deep learning schemes but achieves near-optimal performance. On spatially-correlated channels, it achieves the same error rate as the next-best learning scheme (OAMPNet) at 2.5 dB lower SNR and with at least 10× less computational complexity. MMNet is also 4–8 dB better overall than a classic linear scheme like the MMSE detector."* DetNet cited at *"on the order of 1–10M trainable parameters"*; OAMPNet at 10 layers × 2 parameters with a matrix inverse per layer; MMNet-iid at 20 parameters total. Complexity: `O(N_r²)` (no matrix inversions) vs `O(N_r³)` for MMSE/OAMPNet; 10–15× cheaper than OAMPNet with online training. Channels: **i.i.d. Gaussian and realistic 3GPP 3D MIMO (TR 36.873) via QuaDRiGa** (Kronecker is mentioned only for prior work — the CDL/Kronecker assumption for MMNet is **wrong**). No latency numbers; simulation-only.

**Exploiting Channel Locality for Adaptive Massive MIMO Signal Detection** — IEEE ICASSP 2020, DOI 10.1109/ICASSP40776.2020.9052971 — [IEEE 9052971](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/abstract/document/9052971) · [PDF mirror](http://dihana.cps.unizar.es/proceedings/ICASSP/2020/pdfs/0008559.pdf) **[search]**
MMNet's conference companion: exploits channel-matrix locality to cut *"the total number of operations MMNet needs to decode each received"* frame; evaluated *"on 3GPP MIMO channel matrices"*. Symbol-level, simulation-only.

## 2. LINE B

### 2.1 Neural receivers for 5G NR (LLR out, LDPC in the loop)

**Design of a Standard-Compliant Real-Time Neural Receiver for 5G NR** — IEEE ICMLCN 2025, DOI 10.1109/ICMLCN64995.2025.11140048 — [arXiv:2409.02912](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.02912) · [NVIDIA Research](https://research.nvidia.com/publication/2024-09_design-standard-compliant-real-time-neural-receiver-5g-nr)
Wiesmayr, Cammerer, Aït Aoudia, Hoydis, Zakrzewski, Keller (EU CENTRIC, GA 101096379). **[full text]** A **CGNN** (convolutional + graph neural network) replaces channel estimation/equalisation/demapping across the OFDM resource grid; surrounded by an RG demapper and transport-block decoder for standard compliance; LS channel estimator feeds it. **Output:** *"the ReadoutLLRs layer outputs LLR estimates for each UE's symbol of all REs on the RG"*; LLRs are treated as logits and fed to the channel decoder; *"the code rate and coding scheme is transparent to the NRX, as the NRX outputs LLRs on coded bits."* **Loss:** *"trained by empirical risk minimization with the binary cross-entropy (BCE) loss that is computed between the LLR estimates and ground-truth bit labels"*, where labels are *"encoded by 5G NR compliant low-density parity-check (LDPC) channel coding and rate-matching"*; optional MSE auxiliary loss on the channel-estimate read-out. **Calibration is explicitly flagged:** *"the LLR magnitudes do in general not match the underlying probabilities… Without additional LLR correction, such as proposed in [16], 'classical' mismatched demapping can lead to severe performance degradation in forward error correction (FEC) decoding."* **Quantisation:** weights float16, **no QAT** — *"Exploring even lower quantization levels such as float8 or int8 precision is a subject of future research"*; **no LLR bit-width or saturation limit is stated at the LDPC interface.**
**Numbers:** 1 ms computational latency budget on an A100; ~350 µs per CGNN iteration + 270 µs fixed init/readout overhead at 132 PRBs / 2 UEs → real-time limited to N_it ≤ 2; Large NRX N_it=8 with 4.4×10⁵ weights vs Real-time NRX N_it=2 with 1.4×10⁵ weights; **SNR degradation < 0.7 dB vs the non-real-time NRX**; approaches LMMSE + K-Best (k=64) at 10% TBLER; 2.14 GHz, 132 PRBs, 30 kHz SCS. **Real-time hardware (TensorRT on NVIDIA A100)**, plus over-the-air measurements.

**A Neural Receiver for 5G NR Multi-user MIMO** — IEEE Globecom Workshops 2023, DOI 10.1109/GCWkshps58843.2023.10464486 — [arXiv:2312.02601](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.02601)
Cammerer, Aït Aoudia, Hoydis, Oeldemann, Roessler, Mayer, Keller. **[full text]** Convolution + GNN, unrolled iterations, adapts to arbitrary sub-carriers/layers/users without retraining; **last stage computes LLRs** per bit, per layer, per RE. Loss: **BCE**, Adam 1e-3; *"training on the BCE was shown to be equivalent to minimizing the KL divergence between the posterior distribution on the bits approximated by the neural receiver and the true one"*. **Key negative result on decoder-in-the-loop training:** *"the LLRs after channel decoding can be used for training if the channel decoder is differentiable… However, we empirically did not observe any gains by doing so."* Reports transport-block error rate close to LMMSE+K-Best at lower complexity, with **hardware-in-the-loop verification against 3GPP conformance scenarios** (4 PRBs training, 217 PRBs evaluation).

**Sionna: An Open-Source Library for Next-Generation Physical Layer Research** — [arXiv:2203.11854](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.11854) (Hoydis et al.; **no peer-reviewed venue found — arXiv preprint**)
**[full text]** Provides the differentiable 5G-NR stack: *Listing 1* `llr = Demapper("app", c)([y, 1/snr])` → `LDPC5GDecoder(LDPC5GEncoder(k,n))(llr)`; *Listing 2/3* replaces it with `NeuralDemapper()` and trains with `BinaryCrossentropy(from_logits=True)(u, llr)` where **`u` comes from an LDPC encoder** — i.e. coded bits are the labels. The `NeuralDemapper` layer stacks [Re(y), Im(y), N0], runs two dense layers, and emits one LLR per coded bit.
**Sionna `LDPC5GDecoder` API (concrete LDPC interface)** — [docs](https://nvlabs.github.io/sionna/phy/api/fec/ldpc/sionna.phy.fec.ldpc.LDPC5GDecoder.html): inputs `llr_ch` as float (no fixed-point type); `cn_update` ∈ {"boxplus-phi" (default), "boxplus", **"minsum"**, "offset-minsum", …}; `vn_update='sum'`; `num_iter=20`; **`llr_max=20.0` = "Internal clipping value for all internal messages. If None, no clipping is applied."**; can be made trainable/differentiable as "weighted BP".
**Sionna RK — Neural Demapper Training and TensorRT Export** — [tutorial](https://nvlabs.github.io/sionna/v2.1.0/rk/tutorials/neural_demapper/Neural_demapper.html) **[full text]**: *"It returns modulation order LLRs"*; loss: *"As demapping is a binary classification task… we use the binary cross entropy (BCE) loss"* (`nn.BCEWithLogitsLoss()`) — **labels here are uncoded random bits**; quantisation: `float16_to_int16 = np.rint(np.ldexp(llrs, 8)).astype(np.int16)` (**2⁸ scaling into int16**); measured mismatch `Sionna LLR range [-143,172] … OAI LLR range [-257,245]; LLR scale factor (OAI/Sionna): 2.42`; sign flip handled explicitly; and *"the LDPC decoder is implemented as min-sum decoder which is know to be robust against mis-scaling of the LLRs."* → **This is the concrete answer to "how are learned LLRs quantised and clipped", and it confirms that LLR rescaling between the learned demapper and the OAI LDPC chain was necessary.**
**Sionna RK — 5G NR PUSCH Neural Receiver** — [tutorial](https://nvlabs.github.io/sionna/v2.1.0/rk/tutorials/neural_receiver/): replaces channel estimation, equalisation and demapping over a whole slot; **CUDA pre/post-processing converting input symbols to float16 and output LLRs to int16**; fixed 24-PRB tiles for TensorRT; slot-based processing. **Sionna — Neural Receiver for OFDM SIMO**: *"computes log-likelihood ratios (LLRs) on the transmitted coded bits… fed to the outer decoder"*; trained on the **bit-metric-decoding (BMD) rate**, `R = 1 − (1/SNMK)ΣΣ BCE(B,LLR)`.
**Sionna Research Kit** — IEEE ICMLCN 2025, DOI 10.1109/ICMLCN64995.2025.11140427 — [arXiv:2505.15848](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.15848): deploys *"a real-time neural receiver… in a 5G NR cellular network using commercial user equipment"*, TensorRT inference, plus a GPU-accelerated LDPC decoder integrated into the OAI stack.

**NVlabs/neural_rx — Real-Time Inference of 5G NR Multi-user MIMO Neural Receivers** — [GitHub](https://github.com/NVlabs/neural_rx) **[full text]** `ReadoutLLRs` = *"dense layer without non-linearity and with num_bits_per_symbol units"*; loss documented in code as *"Binary cross-entropy loss on LLRs"* plus an optional MSE channel-estimate loss (`weighting_double_readout` 0.01–0.02); no LLR clipping/quantisation defined inside the model (int16 appears only at the OAI boundary).
**NVIDIA Aerial / pyAerial PUSCH neural receiver** — [docs](https://docs.nvidia.com/aerial/cuda-accelerated-ran/latest/content/notebooks/example_neural_receiver.html) **[full text]**: the NN *"outputs log-likelihood ratios directly"*, then `derate_match` → `LdpcDecoder.decode` → `CrcChecker`; measured **TB-level BLER vs Es/N0** with nanoseconds-scale per-TB timing (e.g. 0 dB → PUSCH RX 0.1056 vs Neural Rx 0.0917; 4 dB → 0.0066 vs 0.0055). **LLRNet in Aerial** — [docs](https://docs.nvidia.com/aerial/cuda-accelerated-ran/latest/content/notebooks/llrnet_model_training.html): *"plugged in the PUSCH receiver chain in place of the conventional soft demapper"*, following Shental & Hoydis "Machine LLRning"; **loss = MSE against target LLRs** (`mse = torch.mean((predictions[:, :mod_order] - llr) ** 2)`), trained per modulation order — the clearest instance of **LLR-regression training rather than BCE-on-bits**.

**CENTRIC D5.2 / D5.4 (Horizon Europe PoC demonstrators)** — [Zenodo 12731570](https://zenodo.org/records/12731570) **[full text]**: *"This two Neural Receivers consume the live IQ stream coming out of the O-RU and output the predicted LLR values that are then inputted into a standard-compliant LDPC decoder."* KPI = BLER after the LDPC decoder and PUSCH slots/s; live O-RU + PROPSIM F8800A + AI server; **no LLR quantisation/clipping description anywhere in the deliverable**. CENTRIC D5.3 KPIs: *"<1 dB loss with respect to B#2"* (LMMSE + K-Best) and *"1 ms latency processing 132 PRBs"* on an A100.

### 2.3 Loss functions actually observed

- **BCE on (LDPC-)coded bits** — the dominant choice: Sionna library listings, Sionna PUSCH neural receiver, neural_rx (`BCE on LLRs`), arXiv 2409.02912, arXiv 2312.02601, arXiv 2406.18993, arXiv 2603.09644. Explicit quote: *"The training for each receiver is performed using the binary cross entropy (CE) as the loss function"* ([IEEE 10017176](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/abstract/document/10017176/figures)).
- **Soft-BCE = KL divergence to the true posterior** (the theoretical bridge to calibration): `∇_θ L_soft-BCE = ∇_θ D_KL(P_MAP ‖ P_NN) + …` ([Lund thesis](https://lup.lub.lu.se/luur/download?func=downloadFile&recordOId=9235508&fileOId=9238393)); same equivalence stated in arXiv 2312.02601.
- **MSE against reference LLRs** — NVIDIA Aerial LLRNet (the only verified LLR-regression loss).
- **MI / achievable-rate losses** — Donsker–Varadhan bound (Fritschek et al.), max-achievable-rate / BMD rate (Sionna OFDM SIMO; [IEEE 9903445](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/abstract/document/9903445/figures)), BMI ≈ total BCE ([IEEE Globecom 2020, arXiv:2004.05062](https://arxiv.org/html/2004.05062v2)).
- **BER/BLER-metric training** — *"Bit Error and Block Error Rate Training for ML-Assisted Communication"*, [arXiv:2210.14103](https://arxiv.org/html/2210.14103); ⚠ its losses, venue and numbers were **not** verified.
- **Bit-wise losses:** a non-BCE form `L_bit(a,b)=(1−b)^a b^{1−a}` ([Chalmers](https://research.chalmers.se/publication/513449/file/513449_Fulltext.pdf)); symbol-posterior cross-entropy for CMDNet; per-stage cross-entropy for SICNN. **Bit-wise MSE against true LLRs: no paper found (negative evidence).**

**Fritschek / Schaefer / Wunder:** *Deep Learning for Channel Coding via Neural Mutual Information Estimation* — IEEE SPAWC 2019, DOI 10.1109/SPAWC.2019.8815464 — [IEEE 8815464](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/8815464) · [arXiv:1903.02865](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1903.02865) · [code](https://github.com/Fritschek/Wireless_encoding_with_MI_estimation); and *Neural Mutual Information Estimation for Channel Coding: State-of-the-Art Estimators, Analysis, and Performance Comparison* — IEEE SPAWC 2020, DOI 10.1109/SPAWC48557.2020.9154239 — [arXiv:2006.16015](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2006.16015). Training uses a **variational (Donsker–Varadhan) MI lower bound** rather than per-bit supervision. Output type and numbers unverified.

### 2.4 LLR calibration / scaling / saturation / quantisation — the evidence

1. **arXiv 2601.16586** (2026): *"To ensure stable and well-calibrated soft outputs, the computed LLRs are subject to scaling and clipping, which is a well-known …"* — the clearest statement that learned/soft-output MIMO detection needs an explicit LLR scaling+clipping stage.
2. **CMDNet** (TCOM 2021): the DetNet LLR histogram experiment — ~97% of DetNet's LLRs are ±1, i.e. effectively hard decisions, so DetNet's claimed posteriors do **not** yield decoder-grade soft information; CMDNet's own soft output resembles a Gaussian mixture and drives a real 128×64 rate-1/2 LDPC decoder (BP, 10 iterations).
3. **OAMP-Net2** (TSP 2020): LLR formula present (Eq. 29), turbo receiver described, **coded/turbo experiments explicitly deferred** — so its LLRs are never validated in a decoder loop.
4. **HyperMIMO** (arXiv 2002.02750): MMNet uses a hard decision; *"One could also use r_x^(T) to predict bit-wise log likelihood ratios (LLRs)"* — LLR output is an unexplored extension.
5. **Real-time neural receiver** (ICMLN 2025): *"the LLR magnitudes do in general not match the underlying probabilities… Without additional LLR correction… 'classical' mismatched demapping can lead to severe performance degradation in FEC decoding."*
6. **Sionna↔OAI integration**: measured LLR-range/scale mismatch (factor 2.42), int16 with 2⁸ scaling, and reliance on min-sum being *"robust against mis-scaling of the LLRs."*
7. **Deep Learning Aided LLR Correction Improves the Performance of Iterative MIMO Receivers** — Chen & Wang, IEEE 2023 — [IEEE 10246845](https://xplorestaging.ieee.org/document/10246845) · [Southampton](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/481845/) — a network **corrects** detector LLRs before decoding.
8. **Neural Augmentation of MIMO-OFDM Receivers for Universal LLR Reconstruction** — [arXiv:2606.29345](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29345) (2026), journal version **"Learning to Refine LLRs: Modular Neural Augmentation for MIMO-OFDM Receivers"**, [IEEE 11587582](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/11587582) — outputs a per-bit LLR tensor `L̃ = f_θ(X) ∈ R^{B×N}` via an ESCNN post-processor over a primary detector ψ; explicitly evaluates *"modulation scaling"*; **training loss and numbers unverified**.
9. **Learning Quantization in LDPC Decoders** — [arXiv:2208.05186](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2208.05186) — learnable LLR quantiser/companding indexed by bit-width and thresholds (`LLR_(w2,T2) = QCM[w2,T1,T2](…)`).
10. **Data-Free Quantization of Neural Receivers: When 4-Bit Succeeds, Why 6-Bit Matters for 6G** — NeurIPS 2025 — [NeurIPS](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/loc/san-diego/123232) · [OpenReview](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=4NsiKauceB): 4-bit quantised neural receiver *"surpasses LS estimation by approximately 2.5 dB"*; and **Efficient Quantization-Aware Neural Receivers** ([arXiv:2509.13786](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.13786v2)): *"4-bit and 8-bit QAT models achieve BLERs comparable to FP32 models at a 10% target BLER… with learnable clipping and per-channel scales"* under 3GPP TR 38.901 CDL-B/D.
11. **Classical constraints**: internal LLR clipping (`llr_max=20.0` in Sionna); adaptive quantisation *"applies a scaling to the log-likelihood ratios (LLRs) and messages"* ([UNC thesis](https://rdu.unc.edu.ar/bitstream/handle/11086/1463/Morero_Tesis.pdf)); *"the least amount of saturation occurs for a given application, as oversaturation…"* ([PolyMtl thesis](https://publications.polymtl.ca/10295/1/2022_DanielBowenDermont.pdf)); **Complexity Adjusted Soft-Output Sphere Decoding by Adaptive LLR Clipping** ([arXiv:1011.2113](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1011.2113)); SNR-mismatch-aware / LLR-histogram-compensated decoding ([IET](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/pdfdirect/10.1049/iet-com.2013.0471)).
12. **Negative evidence:** no paper was found that applies **temperature scaling** to a neural receiver's LLRs, and none labelled "LLR calibration for learned demappers"; **no paper gave a concrete LLR bit-width / saturation limit at the LDPC interface** (the only concrete numbers found are Sionna-RK's int16 with 2⁸ scaling and `llr_max = 20.0`); **min-sum vs sum-product for a learned demapper's BLER curves was not resolved anywhere**.

### 2.5 End-to-end learned receivers (loss + LLR handling)

- **Trainable Communication Systems: Concepts and Prototype** — Cammerer, Aït Aoudia, Dörner, Stark, Hoydis, ten Brink — IEEE Transactions on Communications 2020, DOI 10.1109/TCOMM.2020.3002915 — [arXiv:1911.13055](https://browse.arxiv.org/abs/1911.13055) · [IEEE 9118963](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/9118963). **[full text]** *"the learned demapper outputs LLR and can therefore be smoothly interfaced with a channel decoder, e.g., based on BP"*; bit-wise CE loss (*"the channel encoder and decoder are not needed, as the loss function is based on the output of the demapper"*); separable neural IDD variant (sum of per-iteration binary CEs; min-sum acceptable if differentiable); **SDR prototype: 2× USRP B210 at 2.35 GHz, trained over the air, outperforms a 256-QAM + 802.11n LDPC baseline by ≈1.3 dB.**
- **Joint Learning of Probabilistic and Geometric Shaping for Coded Modulation** — IEEE Globecom 2020, DOI 10.1109/GLOBECOM42002.2020.9348032 — [arXiv:2004.05062](https://arxiv.org/html/2004.05062v2): differentiable demapper computes LLRs fed to the decoder; optimised on **bit-wise mutual information**, *"the KL divergence in (11) is null, and the loss function L equals the BMI R up to the sign"*; labels come from a real channel encoder.
- **Model-Free Training of End-to-End Communication Systems** — IEEE JSAC 2019, DOI 10.1109/JSAC.2019.2933891 — [arXiv:1812.05929](https://arxiv.org/html/1812.05929v3): categorical cross-entropy loss; **no LLR occurrence in the full text**; SDR prototype.
- **Towards Hardware Implementation of Neural Network-based Communication Algorithms** — IEEE SPAWC 2019, DOI 10.1109/SPAWC.2019.8815398 — [arXiv:1902.06939](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.06939): fixed-point/quantised weights for FPGA/ASIC; no LLR content.
- **End-to-End Learning for OFDM: From Neural Receivers to Pilotless Communication** — IEEE TWC 2021, DOI 10.1109/TWC.2021.3101364 — [arXiv:2009.05261](https://arxiv.org/html/2009.05261v2): total BCE loss; LLRs from Gaussian/APP demapping fed to BP; **+7% throughput at equal BER** vs the pilot-based baseline; simulation. ⚠ **The title "...to Hardware Feasibility" does not exist** — that is a mis-citation.
- **Interference Cancellation Based Neural Receiver for Superimposed Pilot in Multi-Layer Transmission** — China Communications 2025, DOI 10.23919/JCC.JA.2024-0220 — [arXiv:2406.18993](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.18993v1): outputs an LLR tensor fed to the channel decoder; **BCE + weighted MSE** loss against the *"original encoded bits"*; simulation; throughput gains 19.98%/24.45% at 25 dB for 300/900 km/h.
- **Site-Specific Finetuning of Neural Receivers with Real-World 5G NR Measurements** — [arXiv:2603.09644](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.09644): BCE against ground-truth bit labels extracted via HARQ; **LDPC decoder on a full 273-PRB slot**; site-specific finetuning gives **1.26 dB (2-iter) / 1.05 dB (8-iter)** SNR gain, halves measured BLER vs pretrained, **0.7 ms vs 2.2 ms on an NVIDIA GH200**; over-the-air.

## 3. Bottom lines for the team

1. **LINE A gives no decoder-ready LLR template.** DetNet → symbol posteriors (and empirically near-hard, per CMDNet);
   OAMP-Net/OAMP-Net2 → posterior-mean symbols (LLR formula exists, turbo results explicitly deferred); MMNet → hard symbols,
   SER-only, no LLR/LDPC anywhere. All are **uncoded, perfect-CSI, simulation-only** studies.
2. **If you need calibrated LLRs, the citable precedents are LINE B**: BCE-on-coded-bits training (Sionna, NVIDIA
   real-time neural receiver), MSE-on-LLR training (LLRNet / Machine LLRning), and — where the learned LLRs are known to be
   miscalibrated — an explicit **scaling + clipping** stage (arXiv 2601.16586) or an **LLR correction/reconstruction**
   network (Chen & Wang 2023; arXiv 2606.29345 / IEEE 11587582).
3. **Concrete interface numbers you can adopt:** Sionna RK uses **float16 in, int16 LLR out with 2⁸ scaling**; Sionna's
   `LDPC5GDecoder` clips internal messages at **`llr_max = 20.0`**, defaults to sum-product (`boxplus-phi`) with
   `minsum`/`offset-minsum` options, 20 iterations; OAI's chain used **min-sum explicitly because it is robust to LLR
   mis-scaling**, and the measured Sionna↔OAI scale mismatch was a factor of **2.42**.
4. **Open slots (verified as gaps, not oversights of this survey):** no temperature scaling / scale-factor search for learned
   MIMO LLRs; no bit-wise MSE-against-true-LLR training; no paper reporting LLR bit-width and saturation values at a learned
   detector→LDPC interface; no 2024–26 survey frames decoder-compatible LLR output as an open problem (only CMDNet and
   OAMP-Net2 say it in the primary literature).
